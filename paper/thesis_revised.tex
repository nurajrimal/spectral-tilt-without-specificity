\begin{abstract}
% The result sentences are chosen by scripts/two_tail_report.py. The TITLE and
% the "neither is specific" framing are not: if report.md shows a robust
% positive Delta in either tail, retitle.
A recent account of transformer geometry holds that concepts ``whisper'' and
syntax ``shouts'': concept directions concentrate in the low-variance tail of the
representation spectrum, and syntactic information in the high-variance head
\citep{acharya2026concepts}. We test both halves with the same instrument. For
each tail we ask two questions. The first is about \emph{placement}: does the
measured quantity sit where the account says it does? Under the published
measurement it does in both tails, though the tilt is small and depends on the
basis and the null. The second is about \emph{capacity} and \emph{specificity}: is the
label better recovered from that tail than from the other one, and more so than
a matched control label that carries none of the claimed content? We test this
across eight open-weight models, eleven behavioural concepts and six window
sizes. \num{LoudOpening} (mean $\Delta = \num{LoudFinalDeltaAllMean}$, 95\% CI
$[\num{LoudFinalDeltaAllCILo}, \num{LoudFinalDeltaAllCIHi}]$).
\num{QuietOpening} (mean $\Delta = \num{QuietMidDeltaAllMean}$, TOST
$p \pval{QuietMidDeltaAllPTOST}$), \num{QuietAbstractEq}. Every result is
reported with and without the model that failed our reliability gate, at six
window sizes, at two layers, under length-matched and length-residualised
controls, and with a non-linear probe.
The window size, the primary tests, the equivalence bound and the
multiplicity correction were frozen before the new data were collected (commits
\prerevcommit{} and \revcommit). We also report the methodological problems we
hit along the way: cross-validation leakage between near-duplicate statement
pairs, retention denominators that turn noise into effects, and isotropic
baselines that overstate spectral effects. Each one, left unfixed, would have
produced a confident but wrong result.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The linear representation hypothesis holds that language models encode
high-level concepts as directions in activation space
\citep{park2024linear,mikolov2013efficient}. A sharper geometric claim has
followed. \citet{acharya2026concepts} report that concept directions
\emph{anti-concentrate}: their energy sits in the low-variance tail of the
unembedding second-moment matrix rather than in the high-variance head. A
part-of-speech probe, meanwhile, reads syntax more accurately from the head than
from the tail. The resulting picture, ``concepts whisper while syntax shouts'',
is a claim about where in the spectrum different kinds of information live.

The claim has two halves, and each half has two parts. It says where a quantity
is \emph{placed} (the tail for concepts, the head for syntax), and it implies
that this placement is \emph{specific}: that the tail holds concepts \emph{as
concepts} and the head holds syntax \emph{as syntax}, rather than either tail
simply being where information of any kind ends up. The published evidence
addresses placement. Specificity requires a control: a label that is equally
easy to read out of the representation but carries none of the claimed content.
This is the problem that control tasks were introduced to solve for probing in
general \citep{hewitt2019designing}. A probe that reads a property from a
representation shows only that the information is there, not that the
representation is organised around it \citep{belinkov2022probing,
ravichander2021probing,elazar2021amnesic}.

This thesis brings that standard to both halves of the spectral claim, using the
same estimator, folds, normalisation and bootstrap on each side so that the two
halves are measured on one scale.

\paragraph{Main result.} \emph{Under the published measurement both tails
tilt; neither tilt is specific.} The published picture reproduces when it is
measured the published way: concept directions sit in the tail against an
isotropic baseline, and part of speech is read more accurately from the head in
\num{LoudFinalGapAllNPos}{} of \num{LoudFinalGapAllN}{} models. Both tilts are
fragile. The syntactic tilt is not significant and reverses in the
full-vocabulary basis, and against structural nulls concept directions sit in
the tail in only about half the cells. Specificity does not hold at all. Against matched controls, the head prefers syntax no more than it prefers an
arbitrary partition of the vocabulary, and the tail prefers concepts no more than
it prefers surface length.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A symmetric two-tail test of spectral specificity, a difference in
        differences of \emph{retention} across the spectrum, with a matched
        control for each tail (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}).
  \item Pre-registered evidence across eight models and eleven concepts that
        neither tail is specific, including an equivalence test that turns ``we
        found nothing'' into ``the effect is smaller than $\pm0.10$''
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).
  \item A direct comparison of spectral \emph{placement} (where a direction's
        energy sits) with subspace \emph{capacity} (where the concept can be
        read from), which the original account treated as one quantity
        (Section~\ref{sec:placement}).
  \item Methodological findings that apply beyond this paper, each of which on
        its own would have produced a confident but wrong result
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}).
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Related Work}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Linear representations and spectral geometry.}
\citet{park2024linear} formalise the linear representation hypothesis and the
role of the unembedding geometry; \citet{acharya2026concepts} add the dual,
spectral reading that this thesis tests. Anisotropy in contextual
representations \citep{ethayarajh2019contextual} and a small number of rogue,
high-variance dimensions \citep{timkey2021all} mean that the high-variance head
is dominated by a few directions, which is why the choice of null baseline
matters so much for any spectral statistic.

\paragraph{Probing and its controls.}
A probe's accuracy shows that a property is decodable, not that the model uses
it or is organised around it \citep{belinkov2022probing,ravichander2021probing}.
\citet{hewitt2019designing} introduced \emph{control tasks}: labels assigned to
word types at random, so they can be learned only by memorising lexical
identity. They proposed \emph{selectivity}, the accuracy gap between the real
task and its control, as the quantity to report. Our loud-tail control,
\texttt{token\_id\_random}, is a control task in exactly this sense, with class
sizes matched to the part-of-speech distribution. Information-theoretic
\citep{pimentel2020information} and description-length
\citep{voita2020information} probing make the same point from a different angle:
probe accuracy has to be read against what the probe could have learned anyway.
Conditional probing \citep{hewitt2021conditional} asks what a representation
adds beyond a baseline. Our difference in differences asks the analogous
question across spectral subspaces.

\paragraph{Removing information.}
Amnesic probing \citep{elazar2021amnesic}, iterative nullspace projection
\citep{ravfogel2020null} and closed-form concept erasure
\citep{belrose2023leace} test whether a property \emph{matters} by removing
it. Our length-residualised control (Section~\ref{sec:length}) uses the same
move on a nuisance variable: it removes the linear length signal from the
activations and asks whether the concept result changes.

\paragraph{Steering.}
Activation addition \citep{turner2023activation,panickssery2024steering} and the
refusal direction \citep{arditi2024refusal} are the behavioural counterparts of
these geometric claims. Our steering experiments are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:steering}.

\chapter{Method}\label{ch:method}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\section{Spectral basis}
Let $\mathbf{W}_U \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}$ be the unembedding
matrix. The reference matrix is the second moment
$\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_\ell\boldsymbol{\gamma}_\ell^\top$ over a token set
$\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{V}$, where $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_\ell\in\mathbb{R}^d$
is the unembedding vector of token $\ell$ (the $\ell$-th row of
$\mathbf{W}_U$), with eigendecomposition
$\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^\top$,
$\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d$. The primary analyses use the published
language-subset basis, so that both tails are measured in the basis in which the
published claims were made. The full-vocabulary basis
($\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{V}$) is a pre-specified robustness check.

\section{Placement}
For a unit concept direction $\mathbf{v}$ the energy CDF is
$C(k) = \sum_{i\le k}(\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{u}_i)^2$ and the Gini deviation is
$\mathcal{G} = \int_0^1 [C(V) - V]\,dV$, where $V(k)$ is the cumulative explained
variance. Negative $\mathcal{G}$ means energy in the tail. We report
$\mathcal{G}$ against two nulls: norm-matched isotropic Gaussian directions, as
published, and structural nulls built from the same data, namely per-pair sign
flips for matched contrasts and label repartitions for unmatched ones
(Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}).

\section{Capacity and specificity}
Placement describes one vector. Capacity describes a subspace: how much of a
label can be read from it. For a window of $k$ consecutive eigenvectors starting
at position $s$, $\mathbf{U}_{s:s+k}$, we train a probe on
$\mathbf{X}\mathbf{U}_{s:s+k}$ and report \emph{retention},
\begin{equation}
  R_y(s) = \frac{\mathrm{acc}_y(s) - m_y}{\mathrm{acc}_y(\text{full } d) - m_y},
\end{equation}
where $m_y$ is the majority-class rate. Retention is the share of the label's
own full-dimensional signal that survives the projection. It puts labels with
different ceilings on one scale. Two headroom thresholds apply, and they do
different jobs. A retention value is \emph{computed} only when the
full-dimensional probe beats the majority rate by at least $0.02$; below that the
denominator is noise and the ratio is undefined. Separately, a quiet-tail cell
\emph{enters the aggregate} only if the concept's headroom is at least $0.10$ and
the shuffled-label floor check passes (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}). The first is a
numerical guard; the second, pre-registered, decides which computed values are
reliable enough to average.

The \emph{tilt} of a label toward a tail is the retention gap between the two
ends of the spectrum. For the loud tail
$g_y = R_y(\text{top}) - R_y(\text{bottom})$; for the quiet tail the sign is
flipped. \emph{Specificity} is the difference in differences between the real
label and its control,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta = g_{\text{real}} - g_{\text{control}},
\end{equation}
with a 95\% interval from a paired bootstrap over items (10{,}000 resamples), so
that one resample is applied to all labels at once. $\Delta > 0$ means the tail
prefers the real label more than it prefers the control.

\paragraph{Loud-tail control.} \texttt{token\_id\_random} assigns every word
\emph{type} to one of $C$ classes at random, fixed across its occurrences, with
class sizes matched to the part-of-speech distribution by bin packing. It
contains no syntax but is exactly as recoverable from lexical identity as part
of speech is. Type frequency and word length are reported as second and third
controls; both correlate with part of speech, so they bound the confound rather
than remove it.

\paragraph{Quiet-tail control.} Folds are grouped by statement pair
(Section~\ref{sec:leakage}), so both members of a pair are always held out
together. This rules out the direct analogue of the loud-tail control. A random
partition of \emph{pairs} can only be learned by recognising a held-out pair's
mate in the training fold, which grouping prevents, so it sits at chance; we
keep it as a leakage detector, not a control. A label that is constant within a
pair is still learnable when it reflects a general property of the statements
rather than pair identity: the probe can learn it from other pairs and apply it
to unseen ones. Surface length is such a property. In the sycophancy sets both
members of a pair have identical length (Table~\ref{tab:length}), yet under grouped folds length is
read from the activations of the CAA sycophancy set with $0.97$ accuracy in
every model. We
therefore use a median split on character length as the control: it is learnable,
carries no concept content by construction, and is available for every dataset.
Its weakness is that it can correlate with the label within a pair;
Section~\ref{sec:length} measures that correlation and removes it.\ifsemantic{ A
semantic control is reported as an exploratory addition
(Section~\ref{sec:robust}).}

\paragraph{Probe.} Multinomial logistic regression (L-BFGS, $C=1$, standardised
inputs, five folds), identical to the published part-of-speech probe. As a
robustness check, a one-hidden-layer MLP (256 units) is run on the same windows
and folds.

\section{Inference and pre-registration}\label{sec:prereg}
Models are the unit of analysis. In the quiet tail, concepts are averaged within
a model before testing, because concepts in one model share an eigenbasis and are
not independent. We use one-sample $t$-tests over models and report Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests alongside.

The following were frozen in \texttt{prereg/two\_tail\_prereg.json} and
committed (\revcommit) before any of the new concepts, window sizes or layers
were run.\footnote{The public repository is a single release snapshot. The full
development history, including commits \prerevcommit{} and \revcommit{}, is
archived and available on request; \texttt{prereg/two\_tail\_prereg.lock.json}
records the SHA-256 and timestamp of the freeze.} They extend, and do not change, the original pre-specification
(\prerevcommit).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Window size.} $k=0.10\,d$ for the loud tail (the published
        setting) and $k=0.005\,d$ for the quiet tail (the setting of the original
        concept runs). The sweep $k \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10\}\%$ is
        reported in full as robustness; no conclusion is drawn from any other
        $k$.
  \item \textbf{One primary test per tail.} Loud: $\Delta$ against
        \texttt{token\_id\_random}, final layer. Quiet: $\Delta$ against length,
        mid-depth layer.
  \item \textbf{Multiplicity.} Two tails $\times$ two layers $\times$ two
        quantities (tilt $g$ and specificity $\Delta$) form a family of eight,
        corrected with Holm--Bonferroni \citep{holm1979simple}. A member that
        was not run enters with $p=1$.
  \item \textbf{Equivalence.} TOST \citep{lakens2017equivalence} with a
        smallest effect size of interest of $\pm0.10$ retention units, the
        smallest difference we would describe as a tail ``holding'' a label. It
        is distinct from the $\pm0.082$ Gini bound used for placement, which is
        on a different scale. Because specificity is directional, we also report
        the one-sided non-superiority test of $H_0: \Delta \ge +0.10$.
  \item \textbf{gemma-2-2b.} The model failed the split-half reliability gate on
        the concept side (Appendix~\ref{app:gemma}). Every aggregate is
        reported with and without it, and a conclusion is called robust only if
        it holds both ways.
  \item \textbf{Cell exclusion.} A quiet-tail cell enters the aggregate only if
        its shuffled-label floor check passes and the concept's full-dimensional
        headroom is at least $0.10$. Table~\ref{tab:two_tail} gives the number
        of usable cells per model.
\end{itemize}

the semantic control of Section~\ref{sec:robust}. It is labelled exploratory
throughout and is not part of the corrected family.}

\section{Under the published measurement, both tails tilt}\label{sec:tilt}
\paragraph{Placement reproduces.} Following the published isotropic method, the
mean word-level Gini deviation is $-0.397$, against a published $-0.356$
\citep{acharya2026concepts}. The unembedding second moment has condition number
$2.30\times10^{3}$ and an isotropic random-direction baseline of $-0.1452$. Our
extraction matches the published routine at cosine $1.000$ on Qwen2.5-0.5B.

\paragraph{Capacity reproduces.} The part-of-speech probe reproduces the
published one almost exactly. On the \num{ReproN}{} models whose per-model
accuracies were published (\num{ReproModels}), our top-$k$ and bottom-$k$
accuracies differ from the published values by at most \num{ReproMaxDelta}.
Before any new result was read, a smoke run reproduced the thesis's own earlier
numbers (Qwen2.5-0.5B: loud $\Delta = +0.0264$ and quiet
$\Delta = +0.1704$ / $+0.0844$, matched to four decimals). The null results that
follow therefore come from the published pipeline, not from a reimplementation
that could have lost the effect.

\paragraph{The loud tail tilts.} Part of speech is read more accurately from the
top-$k$ window than from the bottom-$k$ window in \num{LoudFinalGapAllNPos}{} of
\num{LoudFinalGapAllN}{} models, as published. The mean retention gap is
$g = \num{LoudFinalGapAllMean}$ (95\% CI $[\num{LoudFinalGapAllCILo},
\num{LoudFinalGapAllCIHi}]$, $p \pval{LoudFinalGapAllP}$). The tilt is
basis-dependent: in the full-vocabulary basis the mean gap is
$\num{RobFullVocabularyBasisLoudFinalGapAllMean}$
($p \pval{RobFullVocabularyBasisLoudFinalGapAllP}$), with part of speech read
better from the \emph{bottom} of the spectrum in several models.

\paragraph{The quiet tail tilts in placement.} Concept directions carry more of
their energy in the tail than isotropic directions do. Against structural nulls,
however, the effect shrinks to a few hundredths of a Gini unit, and the
direction sits in the tail in only \num{PlacementNTail}{} of
\num{PlacementCapacityN}{} (model, concept) cells
(Section~\ref{sec:placement}, Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}).

\chapter{Methodological Findings}\label{ch:methods}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each of these, left in place, would have produced a confident but wrong result.
We report them because they are not specific to this paper.

\section{Isotropic baselines overstate spectral effects}
In a strongly anisotropic spectrum, a norm-matched random direction already has
a Gini deviation of $-0.16$ to $-0.28$. A direction can therefore look
anti-concentrated against the isotropic null while being indistinguishable from
a sign-flip or label-repartition null built from the same data
(Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}). Both nulls should be reported; choosing the one that
gives the more favourable answer is not an option.

\section{Leakage between near-duplicate pairs}\label{sec:leakage}
Statement pairs share most of their tokens (sycophancy pairs share $99.2\%$).
A statement-level split puts a test item's pair-mate in the training fold with
the opposite label about $80\%$ of the time. The probe learns the mate and
answers the test item confidently backwards, so a linear probe scores
\emph{below} chance ($0.18$ on a balanced binary task in gemma-2-2b). In the
other direction, the same leakage inflates any pair-level control label.
Pair-grouped folds remove both effects; a pair-level random label then sits at
chance and serves as a leakage detector.

\section{Retention denominators}
Normalising by full-dimensional headroom puts labels on one scale, but when the
headroom is small it turns a two-point accuracy difference into a retention gap
of $-0.47$ (Qwen2.5-0.5B on sycophancy, headroom $0.045$). Cells with concept
headroom below $0.10$ are excluded, as pre-registered; per-model counts of
usable cells are given in Table~\ref{tab:two_tail}, and the summary file
\texttt{concept\_subspace\_summary.csv} reports every test with and without the
exclusion.

\section{Degenerate bottom eigenspaces}
When regularisation $\lambda\mathbf{I}$ dominates the small eigenvalues, the
bottom of the spectrum collapses onto a plateau within which the eigenvectors
are arbitrary, so ``the bottom-$k$ subspace'' names no reproducible object. At
$\lambda = 0.1$ the condition number fell to $1.38$ and the random-direction
Gini to $-0.0004$. All reported runs check for this; none of the primary bases
is degenerate.

\chapter{Limitations}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The pre-registered quiet-tail control is surface form.} We
        measure length's correlation with each label and test whether the result
        survives balancing and residualising it.\ifsemantic{ The semantic
        (topic) control is exploratory, covers only the sycophancy family, and
        may partly reflect prompt format.} A semantic nuisance label annotated on
        the same truth statements would be the strongest control and does not
        exist in these datasets.
  \item \textbf{Eight models.} Models are the unit of analysis, so $n=8$ (or 7).
        Effects smaller than the 90\% intervals reported here
        cannot be ruled out at this $n$.
  \item \textbf{Two layers.} We probe the final and mid-depth layers, not the
        full depth profile.
  \item \textbf{Probes measure decodability.} A null specificity result says the
        tails do not \emph{hold} labels selectively; it does not say the model
        does not \emph{use} spectral position. Interventional tests
        \citep{elazar2021amnesic,belrose2023leace} are the natural next step.
  \item \textbf{One syntactic label, one language.} Part of speech on English
        EWT. Dependency relations and other languages are untested here.
  \item \textbf{gemma-2-2b} fails the concept-side reliability gate, and its
        word concepts never separate at any layer. We report it both ways
        throughout rather than resolving why.
  \item \textbf{Refusal} is unmatched and enters only the placement analysis.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Conclusion}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Measured the way it was published, the spectral account holds, if weakly:
concept directions sit in the tail against an isotropic baseline, and syntax is
read slightly more easily from the head in the published basis.
Measured with a control on each side, using one estimator for both, neither
tail is specific. The head prefers syntax no more than it prefers an arbitrary
partition of the vocabulary, and the tail prefers concepts no more than it
prefers length. Spectral claims about representations need the same control-task
discipline that probing claims already require.
